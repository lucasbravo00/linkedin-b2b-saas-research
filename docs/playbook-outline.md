# LinkedIn Organic Content Strategy Playbook for B2B SaaS — Draft Outline

**Status:** Synthesized from 25 LinkedIn posts + 4 YouTube transcripts + 1 research report  
**Last Updated:** June 2026

## I. Strategy: Why LinkedIn (Gerhardt, Kramer)

### The Founder Brand Thesis
- **LinkedIn is the only platform where humans still drive buying decisions** in B2B. Until AI agents exclusively talk to AI agents, real people are involved in B2B buying. (Dave Gerhardt, transcript)
- **Personal brands > company pages.** Company engagement is 10x lower than founder engagement. Employees are opt-in vs. push (inbound vs. reluctant). (Dave Gerhardt, YouTube; Adam Robinson, YouTube)
- **The founder is the distribution engine.** Building a personal brand on LinkedIn is the fastest path to awareness in 2026. (Adam Robinson, YouTube)

### Why Not Other Platforms?
- Twitter: Incredibly competitive for B2B SaaS. LinkedIn is less crowded at founder level. (Adam Robinson, YouTube)
- Instagram/TikTok: B2B audiences not present. Design/video creators dominate; no room for PMM thought leadership. (Pierri, YouTube)
- YouTube: Untapped for B2B *if* you bring credibility, not YouTuber vibes. Takes 2–3 years for momentum. (Devin Reed, YouTube; Reed, post)

### The Bar Is Extremely Low on LinkedIn
- Most B2B content is low-effort, generic, or AI-generated B-minus slop.
- If you put in genuine effort + frameworks + real examples, you win by default.
- "LinkedIn is a meritocracy unlike Instagram. The bar is unbelievably low. If you try a little harder than everyone else, you win." (Pierri, YouTube)

## II. Content Engine: System & Repeatability (Thormeier, Reed)

### The 12-Month Content-Market Fit Timeline
- **Months 1–6:** Post consistently into the void (2–3 likes per post). No lead generation yet. Signal comes from small comment interactions, DM requests.
- **Months 7–9:** Niche down. Find your angle. Listen for signal from audience.
- **Months 10–12:** Content-market fit clicks. You know exactly what to write, who it hits, how it lands. Virtuous feedback loop begins.
- **Months 13+:** Scale and optimize.
  - Source: Adam Robinson (YouTube): "Content-market fit is as hard as product-market fit. Took me 12 months."

### Two Content Formats That Always Work

#### 1. **Spiky Opinion** (Contrarian POV)
- Take widely-accepted advice and show why it's wrong (or wrong in specific contexts).
- Examples:
  - "Customers don't care about your product features" (common wisdom) vs. "They DO care—and you shouldn't hide features behind demos." (Pierri, post 4)
  - "Done is better than perfect" (conventional) vs. "Done is better IF execution quality hits the mark needed." (Kao, post 3)
- Benefits: Creates discussion, memorable, makes you a guide. (Pierri, YouTube)
- Source: Pierri & Rob on Fletch (YouTube): "Spiky opinions form the Fletch way of viewing the world. People either defend the old approach or nod and say 'huh, that makes sense.'"

#### 2. **Super Specific How** (Deep expertise)
- Teach at high detail level. Show your reasoning, frameworks, examples.
- Real company examples (Notion, Calendly, Octonomy) lower time to understanding.
- Combine framework + real example = fast learning. (Pierri, YouTube)
- Source: "Wes Kao's two concepts—spiky opinion + super specific how—have been our North Star." (Pierri, YouTube)

### Operationalizing Content Production (Thormeier's System)

#### Batch Production with Discipline
- **Every two weeks:** 2.5-hour ideation session (Tycho Luijten case, Thormeier post 2)
  - 20-min silent brainstorm (each person writes down ideas)
  - Pitch ideas to each other
  - Pick ONE idea (no going back)
  - 2 hours spent on: (1) script writing, (2) shoot planning
- **Two types of videos:**
  - "Hero video": High production, multiple people, day+ shoot (Thormeier, post 2)
  - "Lean and mean": Phone-recorded, quick turnaround (Thormeier, post 2)
- **Script first, always.** Good idea + bad script = bad output. Script is everything. (Thormeier, post 2)
- **Timers and forced decisions.** Don't wake up hoping for ideas; block time and commit. (Thormeier, post 2)

#### For LinkedIn Post Creation (Pierri & Rob)
- 90 minutes to 3 hours per post (average ~2 hours) (Pierri, YouTube)
- Majority of time on **conceptualizing and writing,** not design
- Many ideas are tested live with clients first → translated to post
- Graphics via Figma (not Miro anymore; more flexibility)
- Daily posting (10 hours/week, often shifting with project load)
- Source: "We're posting from the hip. No detailed calendar. This forces us to stay close to market." (Pierri, YouTube)

#### Using AI to Amplify, Not Replace
- Dave Gerhardt's model: 2 hours per newsletter using Claude as "creative agency partner"
  - You provide: transcripts, context, past examples, background
  - Claude asks clarifying questions; you piece together sections
  - You edit heavily; great writing = great editing
  - Output is better, not faster. (Dave Gerhardt, YouTube)
- "The lie of AI is thinking it can offer perspective. People want a point of view, not AI slop." (Adam Robinson, YouTube)

## III. Founder-Led Execution & Build-in-Public (Robinson, Sordell)

### The Founder as Credible Voice
- **Founder = highest-leverage distribution channel.** Founder posts outperform company posts 10x+. (Dave Gerhardt, YouTube; Adam Robinson, YouTube)
- **Vulnerability cuts through noise.** Share the hard truths: layoffs, down months, competitive threats, personal struggles.
  - Adam Robinson's monthly: "4th down month in a row, down 2.3%, we're in the woods, I don't know when this stops but I know it's not next month." (Robinson, post 3)
  - Response: "How did you even write that?" from his audience.
- **The vulnerability barrier:** Things you'd tell your wife, maybe not employees, but you post to the internet. (Robinson, YouTube)
- **Credibility through consistency:** $3.8M revenue by posting authentically > trying to hide struggles. (Sordell, post 3)

### Build-in-Public Formula (Adam Robinson, 5-Step Playbook)

#### Step 1: Founder Brand
- Post 3x/week about what you're building, what's happening
- Post weekly live show (optional but powerful)
- Make YouTube videos
- Goal: Create awareness before launch (Robinson, post 2)

#### Step 2: Ride a Wave
- Spot macro trends (AI/Claude explosion in 2026) and position yourself in the narrative
- Don't force it; genuinely participate (Robinson, post 2)

#### Step 3: Creator UGC Exchange
- Partner with creators: offer them visibility in exchange for user-generated content
- "I engage with ANY post that tags us from my personal account" → makes creators feel amplified (Robinson, post 2)

#### Step 4: Keep Product Simple
- Obsess over quality, not feature sprawl
- 5 things done well > 50 mediocre things
- Simplicity = defensibility (Robinson, post 2)

#### Step 5: Automate Operations, Not Content
- AI handles customer support, documentation, routine work
- Founder stays close to customers + posting
- Goal: $0–$1M ARR with minimal team (Robinson, post 2)

### Personal Branding Fundamentals (Sordell)
- **Consistency > quality.** 10% quality content, 90% consistency in posting. Most fail because they stop, not because content is bad. (Sordell, post 1)
- **Stop comparing; stop scrolling.** Consumer mindset = low growth. Creator mindset = exponential. (Sordell, post 1)
- **3 killer habits:** No doom-scrolling, no content-comparing (stop being the next Hormozi), no strategy-changing mid-stride. Pick one angle, stick 12 months. (Sordell, post 1)

## IV. Post Craft: Hooks, Structure, Formats (Pierri, Kao)

### The Post Formula (Pierri, Robinson, Reed)

#### Hook (First Line)
- **Stop the scroll first.** Most B2B content is generic; yours must arrest attention in 2 seconds.
- **Format-specific hooks:**
  - Spiky opinion: Challenge or reframe common wisdom (Pierri, posts)
  - Vulnerability: Admit struggle/failure (Robinson, posts)
  - Curiosity gap: Pose a specific problem then walk through solution (Kao, posts)
  - Contrarian: "Everyone says X. They're wrong." (Pierri, YouTube)

#### Body (Build Credibility)
- **Real example from known company.** "Notion does X because…" or "Calendly's homepage shows Y." Lower time to understanding. (Pierri, YouTube)
- **Show your reasoning.** "Because" statements create believability. "Trust me" fails. (Kao, post 2)
- **Teach as if writing to a friend.** Avoid jargon, be clear. (Pierri, YouTube)

#### CTA (Call to Action)
- **Soft CTAs work better than hard sells.** "DM me if you want to talk positioning" vs. "BUY NOW." (Pierri, posts)
- **Ask a question.** Engages audience, creates comment loop. (Pierri, post 2)
- **Link to resource or newsletter.** Give immediate value. (Sordell, post 3)

### Wes Kao's Writing Craft Principles

#### **Delete These 9 Words** (Post 1)
- ❌ Fine → ✅ Great (warmer)
- ❌ However → ✅ But (simpler)
- ❌ Unfortunately → ✅ Direct reframe (shorter)
- ❌ Double negatives → ✅ Positive reframe (less cognitive load)
- ❌ Can't until → ✅ Can [positive]
- ❌ Honestly → ✅ Delete (assume truth)
- ❌ Just → ✅ Delete (strengthens sentence)
- ❌ Very, really, truly → ✅ Stronger adjectives (specific)
- ❌ No problem → ✅ My pleasure (pride in effort)

#### **Show Your Reasoning** (Post 2)
- ❌ "Trust me, I've done this many times."
- ✅ "I'm recommending X because [evidence]. I saw similar play out with [example], so we need to watch Y. This matters because Z."
- Spend 15 seconds to explain rationale = hours saved in friction
- Explicitness = persuasion (Kao, post 2)

#### **Reframe the Cliché** (Post 3)
- Common: "Done is better than perfect"
- Nuance: "Done is better IF execution quality is actually good enough for the goal"
- Avoid using clichés as excuses for sloppy work (Kao, post 3)

### Visual Design (Pierri & Rob)
- **High effort visuals = signal of value.** People save posts that look complex/useful even if they don't read. (Pierri, YouTube)
- **Graphics in Figma > Miro > text-only.** Real framework graphics stop the scroll. (Pierri, YouTube)
- **Don't chase AI-generated images.** Use ChatGPT image generation sparingly. Real examples from companies outperform slick mockups. (Pierri, YouTube)
- **Consistency in design system.** Lock in brand colors/shapes so posts feel cohesive. (Pierri, YouTube)

## V. Distribution & Content-Led GTM (Kramer, Lattanzio)

### The Attribution Problem in B2B
- **92% of buyers already have a vendor in mind when they fill out a form.** Decision made in "dark social"—content, peer chats, ChatGPT searches. (Lattanzio, post 1)
- **Last-touch attribution is a lie.** LinkedIn organic content doesn't close deals directly; it builds preference. Remove it and your best channels collapse. (Lattanzio, post 1)
- **LinkedIn organic lifts other channels:** Accounts exposed to LinkedIn content show 46% higher paid search conversion, 43% better SDR-to-deal, 112% lift in content marketing. (Lattanzio, post 1)
- **Implication:** Measure brand lift, not direct lead attribution.

### Multi-Channel Repurposing (Reed, Lattanzio)

#### **Phase 1: Batch Repurposing**
- Create 3 hero assets per month (long-form video, article, podcast)
- Chop into 3 YouTube Shorts per day + clips for LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok
- Timeline: 36 hours from production to 12+ distributed pieces (Reed, YouTube)

#### **Phase 2: Evergreen Content**
- "Always-on" newsletter summarizing weekly insights
- 1–2 hour monologue on trending topic (recorded at home, minimal production)
- Chopped into newsletter, podcast, clips, YouTube video (Reed, YouTube)

#### **Phase 3: Owned Channels**
- Newsletter as trust builder (weekly, deep-dive, 2+ hours to produce) (Gerhardt, YouTube)
- Email metrics: growth, engagement, open rate, clicks, replies, unsubscribe (Gerhardt, YouTube)
- Podcast as word-of-mouth driver (best way content gets discovered) (Reed, YouTube)

#### **Phase 4: Event Flywheel**
- In-person events (basketball court backdrop, city skylines) = radically different thumbnails
- Repurpose: video clips, behind-scenes (conflict, screw-ups), story arcs (Reed, YouTube)
- "Content about content outperforms the content" (Reed, YouTube)

### Research-as-Content (Kramer)
- **Invest in original data.** Analyze 100+ companies, extract patterns, publish findings.
- **Example:** Only 39/100 B2B companies let you book a demo in the flow (vs. "we'll email you back"). (Kramer, post 1)
- **CTA:** Use findings to sponsor tools (RevenueHero, etc.) → affiliate lift
- **Benefit:** Owned insight, credibility, shareable (Kramer, post 1)

### AI-Assisted Content Ops (Lattanzio, post 2)
- **Newsjacking automation:** Script runs at 6am, scrapes X/Google/Reddit, drops drafts in Slack by morning
- **Sales call transcripts → content briefs:** Drop recording, pull objections + buyer language
- **Community listening:** Point bot at Reddit/forums where ICP hangs out
- **Weekly performance narrative:** Pull analytics, have AI write "so what" in plain language for CEO
- **Content audit:** Map what's covered, what's repeated, what's missing vs. ICP

## VI. Algorithm & Data Foundation (van der Blom)

### The LinkedIn Algorithmic Shift (2025–26)
- **Organic reach: down ~50%** year-over-year (2025 baseline)
- **Engagement: down ~25%**
- **Follower growth: down 59%**
- **The trade-off:** Reach is lower BUT engagement is higher-quality. (van der Blom, Algorithm Report)

### Native Formats > External Links
- **Document carousels (PDFs):** 6.60% engagement (highest of any format)
- **Single external link in post body:** −18.8% median reach penalty
- **Video:** Good engagement, but video is table-stakes for B2B SaaS now (Pierri, YouTube)
- **Text with strategic images:** Still works if design is exceptional (Pierri, YouTube)
- **Implication:** Carousel posts, native video, PDFs should dominate strategy

### The First 60–90 Minutes Are Decisive
- **Cold start:** First comments, shares, dwell time signal to algorithm whether to amplify or suppress
- **Strategy:** 
  - Comment on your own post early (within 5–10 min) to bump it in feed
  - Engage with comments immediately
  - Have close audience (team, friends, inner circle) engage in first hour
  - Design hook to stop the scroll in first 3 seconds (van der Blom, Algorithm Report)

### Measuring Success (NOT Attribution)
- **Vanity metrics to ignore:** Impressions, reach, follower count
- **Real metrics to track:**
  - **DM quality:** Are inbound DMs from ICP or randos?
  - **Email signup rate:** Newsletter growth (Gerhardt, YouTube)
  - **Engagement rate:** Comments from actual customers/prospects (Pierri, YouTube; Gerhardt, YouTube)
  - **NPS across products:** Are community members happy? (Gerhardt, YouTube)
  - **Unsubscribe rate:** If newsletter unsubscribes rise, content strategy is failing (Gerhardt, YouTube)
- **Focus:** Are the right people paying attention, not how many people? (Pierri, YouTube; Robinson, YouTube)

## VII. Weekly Publishing System (Integrated)

### **Monday: Ideation + Planning**
- **1-hour ideas meeting** (founder + content lead/ghost writer)
- Brainstorm from: customer calls, team feedback, market signals, personal observations
- Jot ideas in notepad (Robinson, YouTube; Pierri, YouTube)
- Pick 5–7 ideas for the week
- Draft outlines/hooks (high-level)

### **Tuesday: Deep Work on Content**
- **3–4 hour block** to write + design posts (can batch across week)
- Write first draft (founder)
- Edit + design (content lead/designer)
- Schedule or bank for Wednesday–Friday publish
- Record 1–2 videos if applicable (Reed system: batch record Tuesday–Wednesday)
- *Alternative:* Work with ghost writer (2-hour session feedback loop)

### **Wednesday–Sunday: Publish + Engage**
- **Publish 3–5x per week** on fixed schedule (Pierri, Robinson, Reed all publish frequently)
- **Timing:** Post between 8–10am ET (algorithm gives early boost, gives day for engagement)
- **First 60–90 minutes:** Founder comment on own post, engage with comments
- **Evening:** Scan replies, respond to thoughtful DMs
- *Total time: 1–2 hours daily*

### **Weekend (Optional): Newsletter/Video**
- Draft + edit long-form content (2–3 hours)
- Record video clips or short podcast (1 hour)
- Schedule for next week

### **Monthly: Reflect + Refine**
- Review engagement data (not impressions—actual DM quality, comment quality)
- Identify which format/topic worked
- Adjust next month's strategy
- No A/B testing needed; trust audience signal

### **Template Weekly Schedule**

```
MONDAY:    Ideas meeting (1h)
TUESDAY:   Write 2–3 posts + edit (3h) + record video (1h)
WED–FRI:   Publish 1 post/day (3 total) + engage (1h each day)
SAT–SUN:   Newsletter deep-work (2–3h) + schedule clips (1h)

TOTAL FOUNDER TIME: ~10–12 hours/week
```

### **Scaling Without Burning Out**
- **Months 1–6:** Founder writes everything (build audience, find voice)
- **Months 7–12:** Bring in ghost writer/content lead (handle editing, design, scheduling)
- **Months 13+:** Founder ideates + reviews; content team executes
- **Year 2+:** Build tiers of content (founder-led for big accounts, junior team for starter tier) (Pierri, YouTube)

## VIII. Anti-Patterns & Gotchas

### ❌ What Doesn't Work
1. **Posting without a POV.** Generic B2B advice + no contrarian angle = low engagement. (Pierri, YouTube)
2. **Overediting.** Strip out rawness, passion, and personality. Perfection kills authenticity. (Reed, YouTube)
3. **Chasing vanity metrics.** Post for impressions/followers, not for the right audience. Wrong optimization. (Pierri, YouTube; Gerhardt, YouTube)
4. **AI-generated content.** "The lie of AI is thinking it offers perspective. People want a POV." (Robinson, YouTube)
5. **Shifting strategy every 5 minutes.** Most fail from inconsistency, not bad content. (Sordell, post 1)
6. **Trying to be everyone's cup of tea.** Narrow your audience ruthlessly. (Pierri, YouTube)
7. **Externalizing the link.** One external link = −18.8% reach. Use native documents/PDFs instead. (van der Blom, Algorithm Report)
8. **Waiting for the algorithm to change.** The bar is low. Just try harder than everyone else. (Pierri, YouTube)

### ✅ What Separates Winners
1. **Consistency + 12 months minimum.** Content-market fit doesn't happen in 3 months. (Robinson, YouTube)
2. **Vulnerability + specificity.** "I'm down 2.3% this month, we're in the woods." >>> Generic positivity. (Robinson, post 3)
3. **Real examples.** "Here's how Notion does X" > "Here's a framework nobody knows." (Pierri, YouTube)
4. **Spiky opinion + super specific how.** The two formats that always perform. (Pierri, YouTube)
5. **Founder as voice.** Founder posts → 10x better engagement than company posts. (Gerhardt, YouTube; Robinson, YouTube)
6. **Systematic production.** Timers, batching, scripts, repeatable workflows > winging it. (Thormeier, post 2)
7. **Owned channels (newsletter, podcast).** LinkedIn drives awareness; owned channels drive trust. (Gerhardt, YouTube; Reed, YouTube)
8. **Measure audience quality, not size.** "ICP engagement 75%, unique engagers 85% post-to-post" >>> "1M followers." (Robinson, YouTube)

## IX. Quick Reference: Frameworks & Tools

### **Top 3 Spiky Opinions to Start With**
1. [Your industry's consensus] is wrong because [specific context/counterexample].
2. Everyone does [common tactic]. We do [opposite]. Here's why.
3. If you're [target audience] and believing [conventional wisdom], you're being scammed.

### **Hook Templates**
- "People overcomplicate [X]. There are really just three [options/strategies]..." (Pierri, post 1)
- "I'm tired, tired, tired of [industry problem]. Here's why." (Robinson, post 1)
- "Last Wednesday I gave up on [project] entirely. Today I think it's a $10M ARR business." (Robinson, post 4)
- "This [finding] made me crazy. [Reason why]. [Here's the fix]." (Kramer, post 1)

### **Format Decision Tree**
- **New to platform?** Start with: Numbered Framework, Origin Story, Contrarian POV
- **Want engagement fast?** Use: Auto-Interview, Listicle, Newsjacking
- **Building founder brand?** Focus: Vulnerability Arc, Founder Playbook, Monthly Updates
- **Sharing expertise?** Go: Research-as-Content, Super Specific How, Teardown
- **Scaling?** Batch: Video + repurpose into 10+ formats (Reed's Phase 1)

### **Tools Mentioned**
- **Design:** Figma (most control), Canva (quick), ChatGPT (image gen, sparingly)
- **Writing:** Claude (editorial partner, not writer), Condo (LinkedIn message management)
- **Content ops:** Cowork (AI automation), MCP connectors (data sync)
- **Analytics:** LinkedIn Creator Mode dashboard (engagement, not impressions), custom export (DM quality)

## X. 90-Day Sprint: Get to Content-Market Fit

### **Days 1–30: Build Foundation**
- [ ] Pick one spiky opinion that differentiates you
- [ ] Define target audience ruthlessly (e.g., "VP Marketing at B2B SaaS, $5–$100M ARR")
- [ ] Post 3x per week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday)
- [ ] Engage in comments within 60 min of posting
- [ ] Write every post yourself (no ghost writer yet; build voice)
- **Goal:** 1–2 thoughtful DMs per week

### **Days 31–60: Test & Iterate**
- [ ] Review which 3 posts got best engagement (by DM quality, not impressions)
- [ ] Double down on that format
- [ ] Hire ghost writer/content lead if not already (helps with speed)
- [ ] Launch newsletter (weekly, 2–3 para, link back to best post)
- [ ] Batch 5–7 posts at start of week
- **Goal:** 10–15 high-quality DMs per week, clear pattern emerging

### **Days 61–90: Systematize & Scale**
- [ ] Lock in weekly publishing schedule (3–5x/week)
- [ ] Begin repurposing: record 1–2 videos, chop into clips
- [ ] Test research-as-content (analyze 20–50 companies)
- [ ] Content team handles editing/design; founder ideates + writes
- [ ] Measure DM quality (ICP %?), email growth (newsletter), NPS (if launching product)
- **Goal:** Content-market fit click. You know what lands. System is repeatable.

## XI. Success Metrics (Not Vanity)

### **Tier 1: Audience Quality**
- % of inbound DMs from ICP
- % of followers who are target buyers
- Unique engagers post-to-post (85%+ = healthy audience)

### **Tier 2: Trust & Preference**
- Newsletter email open rate (40%+ is strong for B2B)
- Click-through rate on newsletter (10%+ indicates relevance)
- NPS of community (50+ is strong)
- Repeat commenters (people who engage >3x on your posts)

### **Tier 3: Business Impact**
- Inbound calls booked (attribute to LinkedIn, not last-touch)
- Team hiring acceleration (better candidates from audience)
- Partner/investor relationships (people who discovered you via content)

### **Tier 0: Stop Measuring**
- ❌ Follower count (vanity)
- ❌ Impressions (too variable, low signal)
- ❌ Engagement rate % (algorithm changes; compare post-to-post, not over time)
- ❌ Attribution (dark social is 80%+ of influence)

## XII. Failure Modes (What We Learned from Corpus)

### **The "Known Well" vs. "Well-Known" Trap**
- **Known well:** Smaller, loyal audience who trusts you. Content is meaningful.
- **Well-known:** Large audience, low engagement. Content is viral/clickbait.
- **Trap:** Trying both simultaneously spreads you thin. Pick one lane. (Robinson vs. Sordell, different strategies)

### **Community Death Spiral**
- Charged communities > free (money = commitment). But messaging by committee waters down voice. (Gerhardt, YouTube)
- Solution: Founder involvement (comment on everything, police low-quality) + clear niche + quality over scale

### **The Ghost Writer Handoff**
- Ghost writer works IF: founder writes first 6 months (builds voice), then ghost writer studies that voice
- Ghost writer fails IF: founder outsources from day 1; voice is generic
- Solution: Founder writes months 1–6, ghost writer assists months 7+
