## Post 1 — 2026-06-12
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/finnthormeier_one-strategy-im-dying-to-try-at-an-enterprise-activity-7471171087670874113-wUvp
**Why it matters:** Framework de estrategia: contenido de cada ejecutivo dirigido quirúrgicamente a su par en el comité de compra, distribuido con Thought Leader ads y alimentado por un 'podcast interno' quincenal de 1h. Masterclass de su content engine founder/exec-led aplicado a ABM enterprise.

> One strategy I'm DYING to try at an enterprise software / cyber company is the idea of specialized exec content, surgically distributed to individual members of the buying committee. Here's how it would work. As an example, let's say we sell large cybersecurity deals ($100k+ ACV) into the enterprise / Fortune 1000. Our economic buyer is the CISO, but other relevant stakeholders, influencers and blockers include the CTO, CFO, CIO, the procurement and legal team, maybe the CEO because it's strategic, and all kinds of VPs and Directors within the security team. Let's say we build a list of 500 target accounts. Most companies will already have cybersecurity-related content going out, that's table stakes. But let's say we now take our own CFO and create thought leadership content from her perspective and publish it on her Linkedin. It's not content that a CISO will find interesting, but other CFOs will. Now we do the same thing for our own CTO, CIO, CEO, Head of Procurement, Head of Legal, VP SecOps, Director of GRC, etc. Our CTO creates content that's relevant for other CTOs. Our CIO creates content that's relevant for other CIOs. Our Head of Procurement creates content that's relevant for other Procurement leaders. They're each creating their own content, sharing their own lessons from their own world, posting it through their own Linkedin profile, which would be relevant to THEIR peers. Now we come back to our 500 target accounts. We connect our CTO with the 500 CTOs at those target accounts. We connect our Head of Legal with the 500 Heads of Legal at those target accounts. We connect our VP SecOps with the 500 SecOps leaders at our target accounts. As they're each sharing insights, they're building trust within that specific stakeholder of the buying committee. We can then use Linkedin's relatively new Thought Leader ads to GUARANTEE distribution to the right, individual decision makers at our target accounts. The nice thing about the Linkedin ad manager is that it TELLS you which companies are most engaged with our ads, so we know where we're resonating and can forward it to the sales team. How do we get our CTO, CIO, Head of Legal etc to actually post regular content? By interviewing each of them once every 2 weeks for 1 hour, host an 'internal podcast' with them, ask them about their lessons and insights, and pull video clips and text posts that we prepare into almost-done Linkedin posts for them. All they need to do is look over the posts, make a couple changes, and publish. 1h/week. All the rest (ads, connecting, scoring, outreach) can be done by someone else. How much faster are we building trust? How much easier will it be to overcome all the hurdles and blockers on our deals? How much better are we penetrating our target accounts than our competitors? To me it seems like a no-brainer. No large B2B company is actually doing this. Reach out if you wanna try this.

## Post 2 — 2026-06-11
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/finnthormeier_i-interviewed-tycho-luijten-one-of-the-most-activity-7470797663593013249-MSi8
**Why it matters:** Desglose del proceso con que un creador viral (Tycho Luijten) operacionaliza la producción: ideación quincenal con timers, script primero, videos 'hero' vs 'lean and mean', grabación en batch. Plantilla de sistema de producción disciplinado.

> I interviewed Tycho Luijten, one of the most consistently viral guys on Linkedin, and asked him how they *operationalized* virality. If you're curious, here's their process: Tycho rose to Linkedin fame (what a phrase 😂) when he published a video called 'The Wolf of Wall Street… but then it's B2B marketing'. It went BONKERS, and now sits at over 11k likes and 5m views across platforms. Since then, they've gone viral many times (just check his profile). Their process: 1. Every two weeks, Tycho and his collegue Steven van Marle have a recurring 2.5h slot during the morning to brainstorm and flesh out a video idea. 2. The meeting is between Tycho and Steven, no one else. 3. They usually go to a new coffee shop every time to get outside of their usual environment. 4. The first thing they do is set a timer for 20min. During that time, in silence, they each brainstorm a bunch of ideas. They might come into it with some ideas in mind already, but now they need to write them down. 5. After the 20min timer goes off, they each pitch each other their ideas. There's a bit of back and forth. 6. They now need to PICK an idea. No going back. They usually pick one that they BOTH get excited about, where it feels like there's an immediate back-and-forth, ideas flowing. 7. Now there's about 2 hours left in the meeting. The rest is spent on two things. 8. First, writing the script. The script is everything. A good idea with a bad script is a bad video. They write it together, having rapport with the other person really helps. 9. Second, they plan the shoot. They do 2 videos every month. One is a 'hero video' with higher production and involvement of other people, one is a 'lean and mean' video with lower production and usually no other people involved. 10. For the 'lean and mean' video, which is often just recorded on a phone, they either do it right there, or they block off another 1-2h slot some time later in the week to get it done. 11. For the 'hero video', they plan a proper shoot. Get a location, get other people involved and briefed, get props or costumes, etc. They make a list and an employee organizes everything. Then they block time. For this type of video, they usually need to block off half a day or even a full day. Here's what they DON'T do: Wake up in the morning and hope they have a funny idea. Or go with any random idea that pops into their head. Or improvise. They block off time, they're disciplined, they set timers, they force themselves to PICK an idea and run with it, they go through the effort of writing down the script word-for-word, they dedicate separate time to shoot and record each video. Tycho has a similar process for his educational videos. He blocks off 2 hours every second Sunday to record 4 videos. He writes the scripts for those 4 videos the Saturday before. Records it with his videographer, which gives him two educational posts to schedule out every week.

## Post 3 — 2026-06-10
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/finnthormeier_how-emily-kramer-uses-claude-code-to-write-activity-7470408576868720640-QY1H
**Why it matters:** Listicle que hace ingeniería inversa de cómo Emily Kramer usa Claude Code/IA para producir el newsletter de MKT1. Formato 'cómo X hace Y paso a paso' + cross-reference a otra experta del corpus.

> How Emily Kramer uses Claude Code to write her MKT1 newsletter (82k subs): 1. ask it to analyze / research large amount of raw input to generate data for a report (eg. crawling the website of 100s of B2B startups for a recent piece). 2. ask it to structure her notes if they're a mess. 3. she'll write the first draft of a section, then ask it to better structure it, clean it up, or write a paragraph more tightly (sometimes). 4. she built a copy editing skill that'll do the final edit / review once she finished writing (she also always has two humans proof read it). 5. ask it to add links: backlinks to previous newsletters she wrote, linking the correct companies or product she mentions, affiliate links. 6. have it write the subject line - she actually said it made her better at subject lines because Claude doesn't get self-conscious about just leading with the strongest statement. 7. because MKT1 now has an MCP server, she often builds Claude Skills around the topics/frameworks of the newsletter and releases them alongside it, almost as mini tools for readers to use, which makes her newsletters more valuable, but also more time intensive. 8. Figma connector via Claude to write the headlines for her diagrams or update all diagrams if the underlying data changes (the diagrams themselves she still creates herself). What she doesn't use Claude for: - writing the first full draft of her newsletter.
